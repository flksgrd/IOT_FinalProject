\section{Pain -- the problem we solve}
\label{sec:pain}

A regular kitchen trash bin is a surprisingly bad piece of equipment. Three pains repeat in almost every household:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bags overflow before anyone notices.} People keep pressing trash down into a bag long after it should have been changed. The bag tears, the lid no longer closes, and the smell gets into the cabinet.
    \item \textbf{Tying a stuffed bag is a hassle.} A full kitchen bag has to be tied by hand, which is messy, occasionally leaks and tends to be the part of the chore people put off the longest.
    \item \textbf{Bag inventory is invisible.} Nobody actually knows how many bags are left in the drawer. You either find out at the worst possible moment (right after taking out a full bag) or you over-buy and pile up rolls in a cupboard.
\end{itemize}

The pains are small individually, but they happen daily and they are felt by everyone in the household. From a product perspective the opportunity is to make the chore disappear: the bin should notice when it is full, close the bag itself, tell the user about it on their phone, and keep track of how many bags are left without anyone having to count them.
